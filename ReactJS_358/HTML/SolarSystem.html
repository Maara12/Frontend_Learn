SOLAR SYSTEM

What is Solar System ?

The Solar System is a planetary system located in the Milky Way galaxy. It consists of the Sun and all the objects that orbit around it, including eight planets, dwarf planets, moons, asteroids, comets, and other smaller bodies. The Sun is at the center of the Solar System and provides the light and heat that make life on Earth possible. The eight planets, in order from the Sun, are Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune.

MERCURY

Mercury is the smallest planet and the closest planet to the Sun. It is a rocky planet with a surface covered by many craters, similar to Earth's Moon. Because Mercury has a very thin atmosphere, temperatures can change dramatically between the extremely hot daytime and very cold nighttime. A year on Mercury lasts only about 88 Earth days, making it the planet with the shortest orbit around the Sun.

VENUS

Venus is the second planet from the Sun and is very similar to Earth in size and structure. However, its thick atmosphere is mostly made of carbon dioxide, which creates an intense greenhouse effect. This makes Venus the hottest planet in the Solar System, even though Mercury is closer to the Sun. Venus is covered by thick clouds of sulfuric acid and rotates very slowly in the opposite direction to most planets.

EARTH

Earth is the third planet from the Sun and is the only planet currently known to support life. About 71% of its surface is covered by liquid water, while its atmosphere contains the gases necessary for life. Earth has a protective magnetic field and atmosphere that help shield life from harmful radiation and space particles. It has one natural satellite, the Moon, and completes one orbit around the Sun in approximately 365 days.

MARS

Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun and is commonly called the Red Planet because iron minerals in its soil have oxidized, giving the surface its reddish appearance. Mars has enormous volcanoes, deep valleys, impact craters, and polar ice caps. Scientists have found evidence that liquid water existed on Mars in the distant past, making it an important planet in the search for possible ancient life. Mars has two small moons, Phobos and Deimos.

JUPITER

Jupiter is the largest planet in the Solar System and is a gas giant made primarily of hydrogen and helium. It has a powerful magnetic field and a complex atmosphere containing enormous storms. Its most famous feature is the Great Red Spot, a giant storm that has existed for centuries. Jupiter also has a large collection of moons, including Io, Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto, with Ganymede being the largest moon in the Solar System.

SATURN

Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun and the second-largest planet in the Solar System. Like Jupiter, it is a gas giant composed mainly of hydrogen and helium. Saturn is famous for its spectacular ring system, which consists of countless pieces of ice, rock, and dust. The planet has many moons, including Titan, which has a thick atmosphere and liquid methane and ethane lakes on its surface.

URANUS

Uranus is the seventh planet from the Sun and is classified as an ice giant. Its blue-green color comes mainly from methane gas in its atmosphere, which absorbs much of the red light. Uranus is unusual because its axis is tilted by about 98 degrees, causing it to rotate almost on its side. It has a faint ring system and numerous moons, including Titania, Oberon, Ariel, and Umbriel.

NEPTUNE

Neptune is the eighth and farthest known planet from the Sun and is another ice giant. It has a deep blue appearance caused partly by methane in its atmosphere and experiences some of the fastest winds in the Solar System. Neptune is extremely cold and receives very little sunlight because of its enormous distance from the Sun. Its largest moon is Triton, which has a very cold surface and is believed to have been captured by Neptune's gravity.
